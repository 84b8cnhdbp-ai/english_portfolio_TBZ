Q3 What tasks do you carry out on a regular basis?
  • Setting up new notebooks for customers (installation, updates, software, user accounts)
  • Handling support tickets
  • Moving our old OTRS templates into our new Jira system
  • Small office tasks, for example ordering snacks for the team
  • Researching and comparing products, for example water dispensers with a fixed water connection
Q4 Which of these is a complex task?

Setting up a notebook for a customer. It has many steps, every customer has different requirements, and at the end the device must work perfectly when the customer receives it.

Q5 Why do you like or dislike doing this task?

I like it because I can work independently and I see a finished result at the end. I also learn a lot about operating systems, software deployment and security. What I don't like so much is waiting for long updates, or when information from the customer is missing.

Q6 How can you organise clear and effective explanations?
  • Split the task into small, numbered steps.
  • Use screenshots for every important screen.
  • Explain why a step is important, not only how to do it.
  • Add a "common problems" section with solutions.
  • Use simple language and explain technical terms in a glossary.

How I manage it

Setting up a notebook in 5 steps

1Check the ticketRead the customer's requirements first.
2InstallOperating system, updates and drivers.
3ConfigureAccounts, software, encryption.
4TestLogin, network, printer, software.
5Document & hand overSerial number, settings, checklist.

I use a checklist for every device, so I don't forget a step. When I'm not sure, I ask a colleague before I continue.

Rules

Dos and don'ts

Do

  • Follow the checklist step by step
  • Install all updates before the handover
  • Document serial numbers and settings
  • Ask when the requirements are unclear
  • Encrypt the hard drive (e.g. BitLocker)

Don't

  • Skip steps because you're in a hurry
  • Hand over a device without testing it
  • Write passwords on sticky notes
  • Guess what the customer wants
  • Use your own account on a customer device

Consequences

What if I forget a step?

Choose a step to see what can go wrong.

If I forget encryption, the customer's data isn't protected when the notebook is lost or stolen.

In the end, the customer is unhappy, and fixing the problem takes much more time than doing it right the first time.
